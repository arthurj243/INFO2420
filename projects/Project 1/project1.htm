Web Site Critiques

Comapany Name: Pitchfork
Web Site Selected: https://pitchfork.com/

Questions:

  1. Loading the webpage up, I first notice the modern, sleek organization of the page itself. I also notice the various ads spread throughout it. My next thought was that I was somewhat confused about what I was looking at in each section. Perhpas they could be labeled better.
  2. This page opts into a scheme that divides each section vertically, whith the first section have a sidebar that houses links to their latest publications.
  3. The site structure makes sense once you've had some time to take in everything you're seeing. I think the first section of the page could be better labeled but I understand it's most likely what the company wants you to focus on, with other sections being supplementary.
  4. Seeing as this site is dedicated to music and news pertaining to it, I think the graphics ultimately improve the web site.
  5. The homepage loaded quickly, but some advertisements lagged behind. Depending on the selected ad, and it's size, this would sometimes move certain aspects of the web page around, which could be confusing or jarring for users.
  6. I think text size and color, as well as background are appropriate and clear. The background is a solid white color and helps the user focus on the content, rather than the web page itself.
  7. This site does have a naviation bar located at the bottom of the header. It works well but could be improved by making it clear to the user which page their currently viewing.
  8. The site is successful in it's ability to stay consistent in color scheme, layout, and feel.
  9. When resizing the window, the web page does a fantastic job at dynamically changing the layout of links, sidebars, and ads.
  10. Something unusual this site offers is the implementation of two forms of navigation. You find one navigation bar at the top of the page and another in a hamburger menu on the top left. This could be helpful when accomadating users that are used to different navigation types, but is ultimately redundant.
